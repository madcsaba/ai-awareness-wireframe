Business case

Why AI Energy Transparency Is a Win-Win for Providers

This isn't an activism ask — it's an argument that disclosure and efficiency work are the same work, already underway inside most AI companies.

Every major provider is already racing to cut inference cost through smaller models, smarter routing, and better batching. The ask here is narrow: publish honest numbers about that work, and put a version of that choice in front of users.

1

Trust & differentiation

Disclosure is uneven across the industry today. The provider that discloses first and clearly becomes the reference point every comparison is measured against.

2

Efficiency work serves margin and emissions at once

Inference is now the dominant cost AND the dominant energy draw. Routing, batching and reasoning-budget controls lower both at the same time — no tradeoff to manage.

3

Procurement & regulatory readiness

Enterprise RFPs increasingly ask for carbon-per-query metrics. Building a clean disclosure process now means answering from readiness, not scrambling later.

4

Risk reduction

Vague “green AI” claims invite scrutiny. Real, audited numbers — including the parts that aren’t flattering — are a defensible position that marketing language never is.

5

Direct infrastructure savings

Eco mode, model routing and a batch queue shift real load off expensive, low-latency serving — the user-facing feature and the backend cost structure point the same way.

Four low-risk first steps

1. Publish one page stating your current methodology for estimating per-request energy use.

2. Add one line to your model card per model, disclosing training-run CO₂e.

3. Surface model size and reasoning-depth choice in your main interface, not a settings submenu.

4. Pilot a “not urgent” option on one feature where latency doesn’t matter.
