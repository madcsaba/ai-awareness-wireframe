Petition

Transparent, User-Controllable AI Energy Use

Nov 10, 2026

To: the leading AI companies building consumer and enterprise AI products

We, the undersigned, use AI tools every day — to write, to search, to code, to learn. We are not asking you to stop building. We are asking for two simple things: tell us honestly what each answer costs in energy, and give us the tools to choose a lighter option when we want one. Right now we have neither.

Why this matters now

Every time we send a prompt, a server somewhere does real work and uses real electricity. That part is unavoidable, and reasonable. What isn't reasonable is that, in 2026, there's still no standard way to know how much — or to choose less when a smaller, lighter tool would do the job just as well.

Some companies have started publishing partial numbers. None of them use the same units, the same assumptions, or the same level of detail, so we can't compare them even if we wanted to. Most companies have published nothing at all.

Meanwhile, every major product defaults to running the biggest, most powerful model available, for every task, even a one-line email. We are never asked whether we'd prefer something lighter. We are never told what the heavier option actually costs.

Our demands

  1. Publish a standard energy label for every model you offer. One unit (watt-hours), one shared methodology, independently audited — the way nutrition labels or fuel-economy ratings work, so we can actually compare one provider to another.
  2. Show us an honest estimate before we send a request. A range, not a fake-precise single number, so we know roughly what a given task will cost before we commit to it.
  3. Give us a real choice of model size and "thinking" depth in plain sight, not in a hidden settings menu. If a lighter, faster option exists for a simple task, let us pick it in one click.
  4. Offer a "not urgent" option that we can choose ourselves. Let us say a task can wait, so it runs when the system can do it most efficiently, instead of always optimizing for instant delivery.
  5. Hold every major provider to the same standard. A commitment from one company we already trust means little if the next company we use discloses nothing at all. We're asking for an industry norm, not a marketing advantage for whoever moves first.

We are not asking any single company to go it alone. We are asking the whole industry to agree on one honest, comparable standard — together.

Why your signature matters

We aren't the ones who build these systems, but we are the ones who use them, every day, in numbers large enough to matter. A single user can't demand a standardized energy label. Hundreds of thousands of users, saying the same thing at once, are the kind of signal that shows up in a product roadmap. Every signature here is one more data point telling these companies that transparency and real user choice aren't a niche request — they're what we expect as a baseline.
